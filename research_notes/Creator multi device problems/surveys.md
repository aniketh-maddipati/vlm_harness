# Quantified surveys of photographer, videographer, and creator problems, 2020–2026

Scope: published surveys and occupational statistics, 2020 through 2026, that put a number on time, money, tool use, failure, or a ranked problem list for still photographers, videographers, or camera-using creators. A figure from a company that sells the remedy is marked **vendor-sponsored**. Chart images whose percentages did not survive as text are not invented. Nothing below is a product recommendation.

Association and government sources that were looked for and not found as in-window ranked-pain surveys: a published ASMP or APA pain ranking; an NPPA member survey that ranks workflow problems; a Pixellu, Hedge, Frame.io, Blackmagic, or Atomos report with a stated sample and instrument; a Patreon or Deloitte creator survey that measures capture devices or post-production. Those absences are gaps, not evidence that the problems do not exist.

## Which surveys rank photographers' or videographers' top problems, and what are the top items with sample size and year?

### Takeaway
The only large, repeated ranking of photographers' "biggest challenge" in this window is Zenfolio's State of the Photography Industry series, and it ranks getting clients and setting prices, not file handling. Independent and association-adjacent studies rank income, discrimination, risk, and mental health. Creator-economy surveys rank consistency, reach, and algorithms. No retrieved survey ranks videographers' production problems with a stated sample.

### Cited Findings

Vendor-sponsored** (Zenfolio sells websites, galleries, and client tools; the 2024 wave was also distributed by Format, ShootProof, SLR Lounge, Professional Photographers of America, and Miller's Professional Imaging). n = 7,646 people in 102 countries. Geography of respondents: 73% United States, 4.8% Canada (78% United States + Canada), 7.9% United Kingdom, 2.68% Australia; California, Texas, New York, and Florida were 28% of the North American respondents. Age: 77% were 40 or older; 28.3% were 60 or older. Self-employed full-time 47.9%, part-time self-employed 42.3%, full-time employees 3.2%. Question, as published: client-based photographers' "biggest challenge" in 2023 was finding new clients, **46.4%**, then determining price points, **21.2%**. Non-client-based photographers (landscape / fine art): selling products **31.8%**, finding new clients **23.6%**. The rest of that ranked list was in a chart and is not in the page text. — [Zenfolio 2024 report](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/); sample size also in [Zenfolio press release, 11 April 2024](https://zenfolio.com/press/zenfolio-2024-state-of-the-photography-industry-report/)
- Same 2024 wave, other quantified pressures, not the "biggest challenge" item: nearly a quarter reported cost increases of 6–10%; business "about the same or busier than expected" rose to 51% from 47% the year before; closures 0.95% versus 3% the prior year; new openings steady at 5.7%; 71% expected the rest of 2024 to stay steady or get busier, down from 75% looking into 2023; 12.4% expected a slowdown versus 7.2% a year earlier. Full-time self-employed wedding photographers: 23.3% reported 2023 gross revenue over $100,000. Part-time landscape: 74% earned under $10,000 from that specialty. — [Zenfolio 2024 report](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)

The Zenfolio blog overview says **over 4,500 photographers in more than 70 countries**; that exact n is not repeated on the report page itself. Published ranking sentence: "In 2024, as in previous years, most photographers reported that finding new clients was their biggest challenge." No new percentage for that item is in the text. Cost finding: **65–77%** reported higher business costs in 2024, and price increases had not kept pace. Respondents mostly in the US (**68%**), then UK, Canada, Australia. **88%** called themselves professionals; **12%** hobbyists and students. Of those earning from photography, **94%** self-employed. ### Inferences
- Where a survey actually forces a single "biggest challenge," the winner for client photographers is acquisition and price (Zenfolio 2024; HoneyBook 2026 says the biggest *workflow* challenge is booking logistics). File organization does not appear in those published winner slots.
- The Zenfolio series is the deepest repeated photographer sample, and it is a convenience sample recruited by companies that sell business tools to photographers. Shares should not be read as the U.S. or world photographer population.
- "Creator" surveys (Epidemic Sound, Linktree) and "photographer" surveys are different populations. Monetizing social creators rank distribution and algorithms. Working and aspiring photographers in the Zenfolio/academic sets rank clients, rates, and, in the academic set, equity and safety.

### Gaps
- No retrieved NPPA, ASMP, or APA instrument from 2020–2026 publishes a ranked list of working photographers' or videographers' operational problems. ASMP's 2023 call was a copyright-registration survey of illustrators, graphic artists, and fine artists; the 2026 ASMP Copyright & Income Survey (copyright, income, AI, costs) was closed 18 September 2026 and no results page was found. — [ASMP 2023 call](https://www.asmp.org/copyright/2023-survey-of-illustrators-graphic-artists-and-fine-artists-about-copyright-registration-issues/); [ASMP 2026 survey notice](https://www.asmp.org/advocacy/asmp-members-take-the-2026-copyright-income-survey-by-september-18/)
- No videographer-specific ranked pain list with n, year, and geography was found, other than the Datacolor webinar poll below, which is not a ranking of problems.
- Zenfolio 2023 and 2025 chart percentages for the full challenge list, and the 2023 time-use ranking, were not available as text.
- Picfair 2024 and the Linktree PDF's exact question text and geography were not recovered.

## What is measured about ingest, culling, offload, storage, multicam, drone footage, or phone footage? Numbers only, with the question wording when you can find it.

### Takeaway
Almost nothing in a non-vendor, probability, or association survey measures ingest time, offload failure, card storage, multicam sync, or drone/phone footage failure. What exists is (1) Zenfolio equipment-use and still-image AI-feature-use, (2) a Format narrative that editing is the task photographers most want to spend less time on, without a percentage, and (3) vendor or consumer polls about culling time and phone storage that do not sample working camera crews.

### Cited Findings
- **Culling as a disliked task, not a timed measurement.** Format/Zenfolio 2023 page, question paraphrased by the authors: top three tasks they spend the most time on, and the task they would like to spend less time on. "Editing was the clear winner." Sorting, culling, and post-production editing are named in prose as the after-shoot work. No hours, no percent selecting "culling," no failure rate. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [Format 2023](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/state-of-the-photography-industry-2023/)
- **Culling as current AI-feature use, Zenfolio 2024, n = 7,646, 102 countries. Vendor-sponsored.** Among listed AI applications (wording in the report: "the most common applications of AI"): noise reduction **44.6%**, background removal **42.8%**, auto-subject selection for adjustments **39.2%**, skin smoothing **29.5%**, color correction **21.9%**, image upscaling **17.2%**, facial recognition **13.9%**, none of the above **12.8%**, photo culling **12.4%**, image/object recognition **12.3%**, photo generator **9.3%**, don't know **1.9%**. Respondents who had not used AI at all fell from **46%** the prior year to **18%**. **12%** more were using AI sometimes or as needed. This is self-reported use of a feature, not time spent culling and not a failure rate. — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- **Zenfolio 2025 AI use, secondary chart reading.** PetaPixel, reading the 2025 Zenfolio charts: **11.6%** negative toward AI, about **13%** never use it, **32.2%** say AI is part of a regular workflow. Most common uses named: automatic subject or sky selection, background removal, content-aware fill, noise reduction, sharpening; also metadata tagging, photo culling, caption generation; **over 30%** use AI to write social media content. The Zenfolio page text says AI is used for noise reduction, background removal, metadata tagging, photo culling, captions, SEO, and social copy, and that usage "aligns with the needs of their genre," but it does not print those percentages. **Vendor-sponsored** underlying survey; PetaPixel is a secondary reading. — [PetaPixel, 17 April 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/04/17/2025-state-of-photography-more-pros-are-using-smartphones-and-drones/); [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/)
- **Phone versus dedicated camera for video, Zenfolio 2024. Vendor-sponsored.** "Preference for DSLR/Mirrorless cameras in video recording": **61.9%** in 2024, up 5.5 points from **56.4%** in 2023. Phone-camera recording **14.2%**, down from **16.9%** in 2023. Camcorders **4%**. Still-camera primary tool in the 2023 wave (Format write-up): DSLR **52%**, mirrorless **41%** (authors say mirrorless was up 50.7% from the prior wave; that reads as a relative jump, and the page does not give the prior-wave mirrorless share). — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/); [Format 2023](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/state-of-the-photography-industry-2023/)
- **Drones and phones as equipment used, Zenfolio 2025. Vendor-sponsored.** The report's own sentences: the equipment question changed "this year," allowing multiple selections, and drone photography was asked for the first time. "Drone photography is now more commonly used than phone cameras among professional photographers." A footnote says drones and multi-select were newly added and that the year-over-year comparison "excludes drones and applies a weighted distribution." Exact drone and phone shares are in charts. PetaPixel's chart reading: just over **17%** use drones in the workflow; combining a traditional camera with a smartphone reached nearly **15%**, from about **5%** the year before; a majority use a single camera, with two cameras "a reasonably close second." Because the question changed to multi-select, the 5% to 15% comparison is not a clean behavioral trend. — [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/); [PetaPixel, 17 April 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/04/17/2025-state-of-photography-more-pros-are-using-smartphones-and-drones/)
- **Camera-plus-phone by age, Zenfolio 2024, when the published breakouts look single-choice. Vendor-sponsored.** "Combination of camera and phone": 17 or younger **8.8%** (phone only also 8.8%; n in that band is small — 0.6% of the whole sample was 17 or younger), ages 18–20 **18%** (phone only **10%**), 21–29 **7.2%** (phone only 3.8%), 30–39 **5.8%** (phone only 0.5%), 40–49 **4.5%** (phone only 0.3%), 50–59 **6.4%** (phone only 0.2%), 60 or older **10.4%** (phone only 0.6%). An overall camera-plus-phone share is not printed. Phone or camera-plus-phone was "particularly popular" for landscape versus other specialties; that specialty share is not printed. — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- **Narrative Select, 2022 survey, as described in a June 2023 product story. Vendor-sponsored** (Narrative sells a culling app, and the survey is the company's). Claimed instrument result: **33%** of photographers use Narrative Select to select images, placed between Adobe Lightroom Classic **40%** and Photo Mechanic **30%**; **18%** of surveyed photographers edit and cull on Windows. A separate sentence is the founder's attribution, not a tabulated survey share: Select reduces culling time "by at least a half." Sample size, geography, and question text are not in the article. Do not treat 33% or the time cut as an independent measurement. — [PetaPixel, 20 June 2023](https://petapixel.com/2023/06/20/narrative-select-is-launching-its-ai-culling-app-for-windows/)
- **GoPro / OnePoll, consumer photo-library poll, not working photographers. Vendor-sponsored** (GoPro sells cameras). OnePoll online sample **4,031** across the United Kingdom, United States, Australia, France, Germany, India, Japan, and Korea. Field year is **not stated** in the article retrieved; the article discusses "prior to the pandemic," so it is not clearly inside 2020–2026. - **Aftershoot "2025" photography workflow page. Vendor-sponsored, methodology not stated** (Aftershoot sells AI culling and editing). The page says photographers spend 4–6 hours editing per hour of shooting and quotes 81%, 60%, 90%, 68%, and 1% client-concern figures. No n, geography, or question text is on the page retrieved. Excluded as a measurement. — [Aftershoot industry page](https://aftershoot.com/resources/photography-industry-report-2025/)
- BLS lists "archive and manage imagery" as a typical duty and says digital capture is transferred to a computer and then processed. It publishes no time, failure rate, or storage volume. — [BLS photographers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/photographers.htm)

### Inferences
- The only culling number that comes from a large stated sample is Zenfolio's **12.4%** who say they use AI for photo culling (2024), and that is adoption of a feature, behind retouching and selection tools. It is not evidence that culling is the top time sink, and it is not evidence that it is solved.
- Drone and phone figures in the 2025 Zenfolio wave are equipment-in-the-business, after a question change. They are not offload-failure rates and not "footage from this job could not be organized."
- Consumer phone-roll fatigue (GoPro/OnePoll) and working-photographer business surveys are different measurements. Using one to stand in for the other overstates what was asked.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study states a question such as hours to ingest, percent of shoots with an offload failure, percent of cards that filled, multicam sync failure, or hours to organize drone or phone footage, with n and year, for working photographers or videographers.
- Hedge, Frame.io, Atomos, Blackmagic, and Pixellu were not found with a methodology section in this search.
- Suite 48 Analytics, "Pro Photographers and their Camera Use," n = 881 professionals in North America and Europe, is summarized by DPReview (11% use a phone for about half of professional images; 31% use a phone more than 12 months earlier; 58% use the phone for their own business photos; 20% behind the scenes; 86% of phone users on iPhone). The article retrieved does not give the survey year, so it is not counted inside 2020–2026. — [DPReview summary](https://www.dpreview.com/news/0107410682/study-smartphone-camera-use-on-the-rise-among-pro-photographers/)

## Do any surveys measure demand for video color grading, video masking, or video object removal, and do they record a preferred workflow or only agreement that the capability matters?

### Takeaway
No survey retrieved for 2020–2026 measures demand for video color grading, video masking, or video object removal, and none records a preferred workflow versus mere agreement that the capability matters. The nearest numbers are a color-chart vendor's webinar poll about how people match cameras today, and still-photo AI feature use.

### Cited Findings
- **Datacolor webinar poll, published 23 January 2024. Vendor-sponsored** (Datacolor was launching Spyder Checkr Video; the poll was questions to webinar attendees). The article says the survey **is not representative**. Interest pool **625** people, webinars in Germany and the UK on DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut, and Premiere, about **30%** professional and **70%** amateur. Question on number of camera systems in a film project: **25%** use only one; the rest regularly use more than one. How they color-match footage: **60%** purely by eye, **12%** with look-up tables, **28%** with color reference cards. Automatic white balance: **56%** always, **39%** sometimes, **6%** do not. "How do you shoot?": majority on standard settings; "only 23%, or 14%, work with log or in RAW" (the article's wording is ambiguous between those two shares). This records current matching behavior. It does not ask whether grading, masking, or object removal matters, and it does not ask which workflow they prefer. — [Datacolor, 23 January 2024](https://www.datacolor.com/spyder/blog/survey-color-management-in-video-and-film-still-has-room-for-improvement/)
- **Zenfolio 2024 still-image AI list, n = 7,646. Vendor-sponsored.** Color correction **21.9%**, background removal **42.8%**, auto-subject selection **39.2%**, image/object recognition **12.3%**. These are current uses on photographs, not demand for video grading, video masks, or video object removal, and not a preferred-workflow item. — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- Adobe's September 2024 Premiere color-management post says "regular conversations with the video editing community" produced a theme of needing to work faster, and that color changes were "directly influenced by top community requests." No n, no instrument, no agree/prefer split. Not counted as a survey. — [Adobe blog, 10 September 2024](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/09/10/adobe-introduces-major-color-management-updates-adobe-premiere-pro-beta)
- Adobe State of Creativity 2024 discusses generative add/remove as a product capability of Firefly. The surveyed items are repetitive design work, project management, and efficiency among design-software users in companies. Not video masking or video object removal, and not a workflow preference for camera footage. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [Adobe PDF](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/cct/resources/whitepapers-ebooks/state-of-creativity/Adobe_StateofCreativity_Report_2024_FV.pdf)

### Inferences
- A claim that "videographers want masking" or "want object removal" is not supported by any counted survey in this search. The Datacolor poll can support only a narrower claim: in a self-selected, mostly amateur webinar audience, most people match multi-camera color by eye.
- Still-photo "object recognition" and "background removal" adoption must not be relabeled as video object removal.

### Gaps
- No academic paper, association survey, or creator-economy report retrieved asked video color grading, video masking, or video object removal as a demand item, a ranked problem, or a preferred workflow.
- Blackmagic, Adobe, Frame.io, and Atomos materials found in search were product announcements, not surveys with a method.

## What do the same surveys say matters more than file handling (clients, rates, algorithms, copyright, AI, mental health)?

### Takeaway
In every survey that both could have surfaced file handling and did rank problems, clients, prices, income, algorithms, discrimination, or mental health outrank it. Several of those surveys never offered file handling as an answer, so the comparison is "what they chose to measure," not a head-to-head ballot against ingest.

### Cited Findings
- Clients and prices beat every other published "biggest challenge" option that Zenfolio printed for 2023: finding clients **46.4%** and price points **21.2%** (client-based); selling products **31.8%** and finding clients **23.6%** (non-client). The 2025 wave again says finding new clients was the biggest challenge, and pairs it with costs (65–77% saw costs rise; only 30–37% raised prices 6–10% when costs had risen 6–10%). — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/); [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/)
- In-person sales, not file delivery, is the 2025 wave's quantified workflow result. Face-to-face versus not, annual gross revenue association: viewing appointments client-based **+20%**, event/sport **+13%**, art and production **+6%**; in-person product sales **+33% / +18% / +2%**; consultations **−5% / +0.5% / −17%**; proofing **−2% / −6% / −40%**; product delivery **+18% / −8% / +31%**. The report says proofing can be done in an online gallery. That is a revenue association, not proof that online proofing is painless. — [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/)
- Pricing benchmarks the 2025 authors synthesized from written responses, not a median from a table: family **$600–$800** (packages often 20–30 photos); portrait **$175–$1,500**; wedding **$2,400–$4,000**; sports **$30–$75**; school **$20–$55**. They warn these are "the most basic building blocks." — [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/)
- HoneyBook's ranked workflow problem is booking speed and scheduling (**36%** and **30.4%**), and payment milestones (**63.5%** of bookings have two or three). The post-shoot integration figure (**5.2%** fully integrated) is reported, then subordinated to the booking story. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [HoneyBook, 24 June 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/24/3316955/0/en/honeybook-study-finds-photographers-biggest-challenge-is-managing-client-bookings.html)
- State of Photography 2022 ranks sexism, class, racism, harassment, disability, and age, then risk and mental health. Mental health and wellness was at least occasionally an issue for more than three-quarters, and usually or always for nearly a quarter. Risk types: health and psychological **38%**, abuse from law enforcement or detention **27%**, assault nearly **20%**, armed conflict **9%**. More than **88%** sometimes, almost always, or always accept risk. On ethics, average agreement that digital manipulation is a serious issue was **60** on a 0–100 scale; staging/posing/directing just over **60**. Consent priorities: photographing minors **48.4%**, vulnerable people **45.1%**. Income figures are in the first section. File handling is not an item. — [State of Photography 2022 PDF](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/34269/1/The%20State%20of%20Photography.pdf)
- Epidemic Sound 2024: the monetization difficulties are reach **53.6%**, inconsistent income **44.8%**, brand partners **39.3%**, algorithms **37.6%**. AI is widespread (**84%** use some AI tool) but contested: quality of AI content **48.9%**, lack of originality **38.5%**, plagiarism **33%**, ethics **28.3%**. Year-over-year drops in specific AI tools, as reported by Business Insider from the same survey: image or video recognition **46.5%** in 2024 versus **64.9%** in 2023; transcription **32.8%** versus **57.4%**; chatbots **32.2%** versus **55.6%**; recommendation engines **32%** versus **53.9%**. The press release states the decreases as −18.4, −24.6, −23.4, and −21.9 points respectively. — [Epidemic Sound, 23 May 2024](https://corporate.epidemicsound.com/press-and-media/press-releases/2024/epidemic-sounds-2024-future-of-the-creator-economy-report-reveals-musics-vital-role-in-content-success-ongoing-ai-integration-by-creators-and-advancing-creator-economy/); [Business Insider, May 2024](https://www.businessinsider.com/new-data-ai-creator-usage-artifical-intelligence-tools-2024-5)
- Linktree 2024: consistency **59%** and algorithms **55%** are the top stated barriers to making money; **70%** earned under $49,000. — [Linktree LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/linktree_black-friday-deal-get-40-off-linktree-pro-activity-7266899700560076800-Jn6r)
- Format 2023 marketing finding, as a contrast to production: word of mouth leads, Instagram leads digital platforms, and photographers invoicing over $100,000 put **86%** of digital marketing effort on non-paid channels. Covid and inflation dominate the business section. Editing is the task they want less of, but the business narrative is clients, pricing, and packages. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [Format 2023](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/state-of-the-photography-industry-2023/)
- BLS median **$44,660** (May 2025) and a slight employment decline sit next to the academic survey's much lower self-reported after-tax bands. They are not the same population or the same income definition (BLS is employed-occupation wages; State of Photography is after-tax self-reports including people for whom photography is not the only living). — [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/photographers.htm); [State of Photography 2022](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/34269/1/The%20State%20of%20Photography.pdf)
- YouTube Culture & Trends Report 2024 (Google / SmithGeiger, U.S., May 2024) surveys fans, not working camera crews: n = **350** online Gen Z age 14–24 and n = **847** online fans age 14–44. **65%** of Gen Z fans describe themselves as video content creators. It does not rank production problems. Included only so it is not mistaken for a creator-workflow pain study. — [YouTube Culture & Trends Report 2024 PDF](https://lp.thinkwithgoogle.com/rs/172-GOP-811/images/YouTube_Culture%26Trends_Report_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Matters more than file handling" is directly supported where a biggest-challenge item was asked and the printed winner was clients, price, or booking. It is indirectly supported where long instruments (State of Photography 2022, Epidemic Sound, Linktree) spend their ranked items on income, equity, safety, reach, and algorithms and do not report a file-handling result.
- AI, in the photographer surveys, is measured as feature adoption and sentiment, not as the top business problem. In Zenfolio 2024, positive AI sentiment was up and non-use was down sharply, which cuts against a story that AI fear is the leading ranked problem in that sample, even while academic and creator surveys record ethics, plagiarism, and manipulation concerns.

### Gaps
- Copyright as a ranked 2020–2026 pain, with n, was not found in published ASMP or NPPA results. The State of News Photography series (World Press Photo / Reuters Institute) documented unauthorized use, but the waves cited in search are 2015–2018, outside this window, and were not re-measured here.
- Mental health is quantified for the State of Photography 2022 respondents and as a risk type. It is not quantified in the Zenfolio business waves beyond the 2026 questionnaire *asking* about wellbeing (the results page retrieved does not give the figures).
- No survey puts file handling and clients on the same multiple-choice list and publishes both shares, so a numeric gap ("clients are X points above ingest") cannot be stated.

## Where do studies contradict the idea that mixed-device organization is a leading unsolved problem?

### Takeaway
The contradiction is not a study that measured mixed-device organization and found it small. It is that the studies that rank problems, and the studies that count devices, point elsewhere: clients and money win the rankings, most Zenfolio respondents are stills-only, and the 2024 gear question shows a camera-plus-phone combination as a minority in every age band that has a printed share. No study in this set shows mixed-device organization as a leading unsolved problem, because none asked it as a ranked problem.

### Cited Findings
- Zenfolio 2024's printed biggest-challenge results are finding clients and setting or getting prices. Mixed devices, ingest, and storage are not in the published ranking. — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- Zenfolio 2025 repeats that finding new clients was the biggest challenge "as in previous years," and spends its new workflow measurement on in-person sales revenue, not on organizing files from more than one device. — [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/)
- Most of that sample does not shoot video at all. 2023 wave: **70%** photographer with no video, **17%** photographer/videographer. 2024 wave: **66.5%** stills only (down from 69% in 2023), **21.1%** both (up from 17.4%). 2025 page: stills-only share up **2.5%** from the prior year and the hybrid share down **0.24%**. PetaPixel reads the 2025 charts as **68%** stills only and **21%** hybrid. Those two readings do not arithmetically match a 2.5 percentage-point rise from 66.5, so the 68% figure should stay labeled as PetaPixel's chart reading. Either way the stills-only group is about two-thirds. — [Format 2023](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/state-of-the-photography-industry-2023/); [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/); [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/); [PetaPixel, 17 April 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/04/17/2025-state-of-photography-more-pros-are-using-smartphones-and-drones/)
- When Zenfolio 2024 printed camera-plus-phone shares by age, they were **18%** at ages 18–20 and **4.5–10.4%** in older bands. Phone-only was under **1%** from age 30 up. That is a minority combination, not a default two-device kit. The 2025 multi-select redesign means a higher camera-plus-phone reading (PetaPixel: nearly 15%) cannot be treated as proof of a sudden organizational crisis, and the report itself highlights drone-versus-phone popularity, not organization failure. PetaPixel also says the majority use a single camera. — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/); [PetaPixel, 17 April 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/04/17/2025-state-of-photography-more-pros-are-using-smartphones-and-drones/)
- Video capture device in 2024 was dominated by the stills camera body (**61.9%** DSLR or mirrorless), not by a phone-plus-camera-plus-drone mix (phone video **14.2%**, camcorder **4%**). — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- AI photo culling at **12.4%** sits below six other still-image AI uses. If culling were the leading unsolved production problem in this sample, this adoption list would be an odd place for it to rank ninth. That is a weak contradiction: people can suffer a problem and not use AI on it. — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- HoneyBook 2026, the one vendor study that uses the phrase "biggest challenge in their workflow," names scheduling after inquiry (**36%**), not media organization. It does say only **5.2%** have fully integrated post-shoot workflows. That sentence is about bridging business tools from inquiry to delivery, in a press release selling booking software, not about mixed-device media management. — [HoneyBook, 24 June 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/24/3316955/0/en/honeybook-study-finds-photographers-biggest-challenge-is-managing-client-bookings.html)
- State of Photography 2022's problem battery is sexism, socioeconomic disparity, racism, harassment, disability, age, income, risk, and mental health. A leading unsolved technical-organization problem would have had room in a long Qualtrics instrument; it is not among published findings. Absence is not a measured zero. — [State of Photography 2022 PDF](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/34269/1/The%20State%20of%20Photography.pdf)
- Epidemic Sound and Linktree rank reach, income consistency, brand partners, algorithms, and posting consistency for monetizing creators. They do not rank footage organization. — [Epidemic Sound](https://corporate.epidemicsound.com/press-and-media/press-releases/2024/epidemic-sounds-2024-future-of-the-creator-economy-report-reveals-musics-vital-role-in-content-success-ongoing-ai-integration-by-creators-and-advancing-creator-economy/); [Linktree LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/linktree_black-friday-deal-get-40-off-linktree-pro-activity-7266899700560076800-Jn6r)
- Picfair 2024's open answers, as summarized, cluster on marketing, cost, and time. One quoted person has trouble selecting best images. The authors do not present device-mixing or ingest as a theme. n is unpublished. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [Picfair 2024](https://focus.picfair.com/picfair-survey-report-2024)
- Datacolor's result that **75%** of webinar respondents use more than one camera system is the opposite of a contradiction if misread: it says multi-camera capture is common in that room. It contradicts "leading unsolved problem" only in that the article treats the pain as color-matching method (60% by eye), and the sample is mostly amateur attendees at a color-card webinar. It does not measure whether organization of those clips is their leading problem. — [Datacolor, 23 January 2024](https://www.datacolor.com/spyder/blog/survey-color-management-in-video-and-film-still-has-room-for-improvement/)

### Inferences
- The fair contradiction is: among surveys that rank what bothers photographers and creators, mixed-device organization is not the published leader, and the device counts that do exist show a single stills camera as the modal kit in the large Zenfolio sample.
- It would be an over-read to say studies disproved the problem. They mostly did not ask. The Datacolor poll is the one place multi-camera capture is common, and it is a biased video audience worried enough about color to attend a vendor webinar.

### Gaps
- A direct contradiction would be a survey that offered "organizing files from more than one device" inside a ranked list and showed it near the bottom. That item was not found.
- No study measured failure (lost clips, failed offloads, unmatched multicam) and found the rate to be low.

## What share of creators use more than one capture device on a job, if any study measured it?

### Takeaway
No study retrieved measured the share of creators who use more than one capture device on a single job. The closest figures are Zenfolio's equipment-in-the-business shares (a minority camera-plus-phone combination in 2024; a majority on one camera in a 2025 chart reading) and a non-representative webinar result that 75% of attendees use more than one camera system on a film project.

### Cited Findings
- Not the same question as "on a job," but the only large-sample device mix with printed percentages: Zenfolio 2024, n = 7,646. Camera-plus-phone by age **4.5% to 18%** as listed in the culling section above. Phone-only is a small single-digit or sub-1% share except ages 18–20 (**10%**). Video: stills-camera body **61.9%**, phone **14.2%**, camcorder **4%**. These categories are not a count of devices per assignment. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [Zenfolio 2024](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2024-april/)
- Zenfolio 2025 changed the equipment question to multi-select and added drones. Report text: drones are now more common than phone cameras among the professional photographers in the survey. PetaPixel chart reading: majority use one camera; two cameras are close behind; camera plus smartphone nearly **15%** (about **5%** the prior year); drones just over **17%**. Stills-only about two-thirds, so a camera-plus-video-camera hybrid is not the majority of this sample. **Vendor-sponsored**; 15% and 17% are a secondary reading of charts, and the question change breaks the year-over-year comparison. — [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/); [PetaPixel, 17 April 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/04/17/2025-state-of-photography-more-pros-are-using-smartphones-and-drones/)
- Datacolor, January 2024 article: **25%** use only one camera system in a film project; **75%** regularly use more than one. That is the nearest wording to "on a project," and it is camera systems, which the author ties to color, not to phones plus drones plus stills. n = 625 interested attendees, ~30/70 pro/amateur, Germany and UK webinars, **not representative**, **vendor-sponsored**. — [Datacolor](https://www.datacolor.com/spyder/blog/survey-color-management-in-video-and-film-still-has-room-for-improvement/)
- About **10%** of Zenfolio 2025 respondents operate across more than one business model (client, event/sport, and art/production). That is multi-line businesses, not multi-device jobs. The report suggests they can reuse equipment across assignment types. It does not count devices. **Vendor-sponsored.** — [Zenfolio 2025](https://zenfolio.com/sopi/2025-april/)
- BLS says many photographers use digital cameras, some still use film, aerial photographers may use aircraft or drones, and photojournalists often also shoot digital video. No share uses more than one device on a job. — [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/photographers.htm)
- YouTube's 65% of Gen Z fans who "describe themselves as video content creators" is an identity share among U.S. fans, not a device count on a job. — [YouTube Culture & Trends 2024](https://lp.thinkwithgoogle.com/rs/172-GOP-811/images/YouTube_Culture%26Trends_Report_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- If a figure is needed for "more than one capture device," the honest large-sample statement is about the business, not the job: in 2024 a camera-plus-phone combination was under one in five even in the youngest adult band, and under one in ten for ages 30–59. The 2025 chart reading still has a single camera in the lead.
- The 75% multi-camera figure should not be generalized beyond people who showed up to a color-matching webinar.

### Gaps
- No study was found that asked working still photographers, videographers, or social creators whether a given job used two or more of camera, phone, and drone, with n, year, and geography.
- "Two cameras" in the Zenfolio 2025 chart reading (PetaPixel: a close second to one camera) may mean two bodies of the same type. The article does not say the question distinguished a second stills body from a phone or a drone.
